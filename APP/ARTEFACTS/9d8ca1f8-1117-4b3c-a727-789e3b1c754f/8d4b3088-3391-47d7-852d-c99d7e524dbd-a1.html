1. Residency Status – The Foundation

Residency determines whether foreign-source income is assessable. Under the Statutory Residency Test (SRT), you are resident if:

  • You are in Australia for 183+ days in the FY, or
  • You have a permanent home in Australia and are in Australia for part of the year with the intention to reside
FACT Your Status: Non-resident from 1 December 2025

1 Jul – 30 Nov 2025: You were resident in Australia (salary on ordinary rates).

1 Dec 2025 – 30 Jun 2026: You relocated to Japan with:
  • 3-year lease (fixed commitment to reside in Japan)

  • Local bank accounts (economic ties to Japan)
  • Only 20 days in Australia (Feb–Apr 2026)
  • Spouse & children departed Australia 1 April 2026
Conclusion: You fail the "in Australia 183+ days" test and likely fail the "permanent home + intention" test. Non-resident status from 1 Dec 2025 is defensible.

2. Employee Share Scheme (ESS) – Vesting & Sale

The ESS has three taxing points: (1) vesting (ordinary income), (2) sale (capital gain/loss), and (3) the US backup withholding (refundable if you file a US return).

Tranche 1: 25,000 Rights Vesting 1 October 2025
FACT Vesting Date: 1 October 2025 (you were resident, in Australia).
Applicable Law: Division 83A–83B ITAA97 (resident employee ESS).

Assessable Income: Market value of shares at vesting − any amount you paid.

Assumption: You paid nothing for the performance rights (deferred-tax ESS).

Calculation (you must provide share price at vesting):

Market value at 1 Oct 2025 = [Share price] × 25,000 shares
Less: Amount paid = $0
Assessable income = [Share price × 25,000]

No CGT discount applies at vesting. This is ordinary employment income, taxed at your marginal rate.

Tranche 2: 25,000 Rights Vesting 1 April 2026
ESTIMATE Vesting Date: 1 April 2026 (you were non-resident, in Japan).

GREY AREA: Whether Division 83A applies to non-residents.

The law states that Div. 83A applies to "Australian-resident employees." On 1 April 2026, you were non-resident. Two ATO positions are possible:

  1. Position A (Strict): Because you were non-resident at vesting, Div. 83A does not apply. The shares are a capital asset acquired on 1 April 2026 at market value. No ordinary income is assessed. (The later CGT gain/loss is foreign-source if the US parent is the issuer.)
  2. Position B (Source-Based): Because the rights were originally granted while you were an Australian resident, and the employment contract was Australian-source, the gain at vesting is deemed Australian-source, and Div. 83A applies even though you vested as a non-resident. Assessable income = Market value at 1 April 2026.

Section 100A Assessability
FACT Section 100A ITAA36 Rule: A beneficiary of a discretionary trust is assessed on the net income the trustee resolved to distribute to that beneficiary in the year, regardless of whether the cash was actually received. Payment location does not change assessability.

ATO's Expected Position:

  1. The trustee resolved to distribute $150,000 to you (Jeff Hall)
  2. You are assessable under section 100A on this amount
  3. The fact that the cash went to Corporate Beneficiary Pty Ltd is a separate transaction and does not override the trust resolution
  4. You may be able to recover the funds from the company or seek trust law remedies, but that is not an ATO matter
The Foreign-Source Income Trap
Potential Major Issue: If you are a non-resident beneficiary, the trustee may not have been entitled to distribute foreign-source income to you. This could render the distribution invalid.

Under Division 6AAA ITAA36, a resident trust distributing to a non-resident beneficiary cannot distribute foreign-source income without consequences. Possible ATO responses:

  • Deny the trust's deduction: The trust may not deduct the $50,000 foreign-source income on distribution to a non-resident
  • Still assess you: You may still be assessed under section 100A on the $50,000, creating a double-tax mismatch
  • Dispute validity: The trustee may have breached the trust deed or tax law, and the distribution is void
🚩 Risk: Section 100A + Foreign Income Conflict

Scenario A (ATO aggressive): You are assessed on $50,000 foreign income as a non-resident beneficiary (even though it may be invalid), creating a tax liability on income you're not entitled to.

Scenario B (ATO cooperative): The trustee made an error; the distribution is void. No tax to you, but the trust's deduction is denied, and the $50,000 stays in the trust (taxable to the trust at top rate, 45%).

Impact: Potential additional $50,000 taxable income, or disputed trust treatment.
