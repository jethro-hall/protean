
# Trusted-Source Literature Base
## Grounding an AI Pre-Assessment Agent for General Practice (GP) Diagnostic Support

**Record type:** literature_grounding_survey (jurisdiction-neutral phase)
**Reference date:** 2026-08-03
**Research mode:** trusted_sources_only
**Governance status:** `partially_verified` — jurisdiction-specific regulatory, licensing, and clinical-guideline layers are **not yet resolved** (see Open Questions). Do not treat this as a complete, deployable record.

---

## 1. Purpose framing

This survey supports building an agent that: (a) conducts structured patient pre-assessment, (b) generates a ranked differential-diagnosis and next-steps briefing for a supervising GP, and (c) records complete decision lineage (source, retrieval, reasoning chain) for every claim. It deliberately stops short of proposing the agent *replace* diagnostic authority — every trusted source found treats human clinical sign-off as a hard requirement, not an optional feature.

## 3. What this survey establishes clearly (cross-source consensus)

1. Every authoritative source (WHO, FDA, EU AI Act) converges on **human-in-the-loop as a non-negotiable design constraint** — "backfill" cannot mean autonomous diagnostic authority without triggering high-risk/regulated-medical-device status almost everywhere.
2. Whether your agent is a *regulated medical device* at all turns on a narrow, jurisdiction-specific test (e.g., the FDA's exact CDS exemption criteria: no image/signal processing, transparent logic, supports rather than replaces judgment).
3. A credible "lineage" architecture (provenance-tagged sources, GRADE-style evidence grading, immutable audit logging, confidence scoring, post-hoc claim verification) exists in the literature **as a conceptual proposal**, not as a validated, deployed reference implementation.
4. Structured, explainable triage frameworks (MTS, ESI) already provide auditable decision-tree templates that predate AI and could underpin the pre-assessment layer's rule-based core, with AI reserved for the differential-diagnosis narrative layer.

## 4. Explicit limitations (per governance rules — not hidden in the record)

- No jurisdiction was specified, so **no licensing, GP scope-of-practice, or medical-device-classification claim in this survey should be treated as settled for any specific country**. FDA/US material dominates because it is the most publicly documented; do not assume it transfers to UK, EU, Australia, or elsewhere.
- Two Tier 3 preprints were included for architectural insight only (medRxiv XAI paper; arXiv primary-care conversational-AI study) and must not be cited as proof of safety or efficacy.
- The lead lineage-architecture paper (Alu & Oluwadare, 2026) is unvalidated by its own authors' admission.
- Primary MTS/ESI protocol documents were not directly retrieved — only peer-reviewed validation studies referencing them.
