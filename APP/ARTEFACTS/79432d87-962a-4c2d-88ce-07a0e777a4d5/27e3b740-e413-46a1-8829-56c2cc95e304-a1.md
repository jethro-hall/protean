**Note on what this layer gives you:** these documents are *blueprints* — they tell an agent-designer exactly which topic domains, organ systems, and physician tasks a real doctor is tested on, and in what proportion. They are not themselves the clinical facts. They're the syllabus, not the textbook. Both are needed; neither substitutes for the other.

## 3. Licensing / Bulk-Ingestion Reality Check (read this before building anything)

| Source | Can a human read it free? | Can an agent legally bulk-ingest/embed it? | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| USMLE Content Outline PDF | ✅ Yes | ✅ Yes, as reference/syllabus metadata (static public PDF, no ToS restriction found) | Public government-adjacent exam body PDF |
| GMC Outcomes for Graduates / AMC Standards / RCGP Curriculum | ✅ Yes | ✅ Yes, as reference/syllabus metadata | Public regulator/college PDFs |
| WHO ICD-11 / CDDR | ✅ Yes | ✅ Yes — WHO publications are generally reusable for non-commercial health purposes (verify specific WHO licence terms per document before commercial use) | UN specialized agency, Tier 1 |
| StatPearls / NCBI Bookshelf | ✅ Yes | ⚠️ **No, if the agent is commercial or produces derivative summaries** — CC BY-NC-ND forbids both | Explicit licence on the work itself |
| Merck Manual Professional | ✅ Yes | ❌ **No — explicitly, by name, for AI training** | Explicit ToS clause |
| NICE CKS | ✅ Yes (NHS staff/students only) | ❌ **No, without a paid Agilio Software / RPS licence** | EULA |

**The uncomfortable conclusion:** there is no free, legal, bulk-scrapeable single source that is simultaneously (a) authoritative hard clinical content, (b) structured like a GP consultation, and (c) unrestricted for commercial AI ingestion. The two sources structured most usefully for a GP pre-assessment agent (Merck Manual, NICE CKS) are the two with the hardest legal walls. This is a genuine constraint on the project, not a technical inconvenience to be engineered around.

## 4. What is *not* here yet and needs direct publisher licensing (flagged, not fabricated)

The following are the actual textbooks used in medical education and board certification prep, named from general medical-education knowledge — **not verified via a tool call this turn**, and I am not presenting page content or citations from them because I have not fetched them:

- *Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine* (McGraw Hill) — copyrighted, no free bulk access
- *Robbins & Cotran Pathologic Basis of Disease* (Elsevier) — copyrighted
- DSM-5-TR (American Psychiatric Association) — copyrighted, explicit anti-reproduction terms
- BNF / BNF for Children (Pharmaceutical Press / NICE) — has a syndication API but requires a commercial agreement (found evidence of "NICE syndication API" for BNF content this turn — worth pursuing as a legitimate licensed path)
- UpToDate (Wolters Kluwer) / DynaMed (EBSCO) — both have official enterprise API/licensing programs, not scraping paths

**Recommended real path forward, stated plainly:** the legitimate way to get Merck Manual-quality or CKS-quality content into this agent is the **official partner/licensing channels** each publisher already runs (Merck Manuals' `mmanualspartners@merck.com` integration program; NICE/Agilio commercial CKS licence via `licensing@rpharms.com`; NICE syndication API for BNF). That is slower than a scraper and costs money — but it is the only version of this that survives contact with the actual rights holders, and it's the only version I'm willing to help design tooling for.
